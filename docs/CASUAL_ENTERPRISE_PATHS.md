# Casual Enterprise Paths

## Design question

How can business be meaningful without becoming the game’s compulsory endgame or a daily operating chore?

## Protected answer

The smallest valid form is a private direction. Profit, scale, premises, workers, and customer count are optional layers.

```text
private hobby
↕
occasional service
↕
tiny enterprise
↕
cooperative direction
```

The arrows are reversible. They are not a level ladder.

## No growth requirement

The engine does not require:

- a minimum age;
- a deadline to launch;
- daily opening;
- endless customer growth;
- hiring;
- a second location;
- maximum pricing;
- replacing old equipment;
- turning a personal project into a product;
- continuing after the owner no longer wants it.

## Valid outcomes

- Keep the project private.
- Offer help once in a while.
- Lease a tiny room.
- Work from a bounded occasional setup.
- Charge a fair fixed amount.
- Let people pay what fits.
- Offer something free.
- Repair old equipment.
- Pause while keeping the room.
- Pause and release the room.
- Pivot to another service.
- Return to employment.
- Close and preserve the history.

None is automatically labeled the best outcome.

## Grounded consequences

Casual does not mean fake:

- occupied rooms cost money;
- equipment wears;
- sessions consume time and energy;
- customers must exist and be able to participate;
- wages must be funded;
- free services do not invent revenue;
- repeated unpaid room costs can release the room;
- closure ends new sessions.

The aim is consequence without coercive engagement design.

## Interface rule

Every major lifecycle choice must state what happens to:

- the room;
- recurring costs;
- enterprise funds;
- equipment;
- source project;
- workers;
- customers/history.

No destructive action should be hidden behind a vague “stop” button.
