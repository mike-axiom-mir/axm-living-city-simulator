# Casual Realism and the Freedom Loop

## Purpose

Casual realism means the world has causes and limits without making ordinary maintenance the main game.

The player should feel that money, time, energy, housing, work, materials, relationships, and distance matter. They should not feel that the game is an endless sequence of eating, washing, sleeping, clicking attendance, and clearing alerts.

## The freedom loop

```text
live an ordinary day
→ notice possibilities
→ choose, ignore, postpone, or decline
→ experience a consequence that follows from the actual choice
→ retain truthful history
→ continue in a self-chosen direction
```

The loop has no universal completion state.

## Grounding retained

v0.6 retains:

- a world clock;
- need decay;
- activity time and cost;
- jobs and wages;
- rent and housing availability;
- event times and capacity;
- material and construction requirements;
- social and authority boundaries;
- autonomous residents who may be unavailable or refuse.

## Pressure reduced

v0.6 reduces or removes:

- strict default player need decay;
- repetitive four-action maintenance routines;
- daily streaks;
- attendance duties;
- missed-event punishment;
- “perfect day” scoring;
- quest deadlines;
- hidden refusal penalties;
- mandatory adventure progression.

## Compressed basics

`gentle_routine` is a two-hour, low-cost activity that provides a modest reset across food, cleanliness, energy, mood, and social steadiness.

It does not instantly maximize every need. It removes repetitive low-value clicking while preserving the cost and time of taking care of oneself.

Players may still choose individual activities when they want more specific expression or effects.

## No failed day

The simulation does not label a day failed because the player:

- did not attend an event;
- did not train a skill;
- stayed home;
- rested;
- earned no money;
- declined an invitation;
- released an adventure;
- left a group;
- changed direction.

Logical consequences remain. Missing work may affect wages. Spending money reduces funds. Ignoring urgent physical needs may eventually matter. But there is no extra moral or engagement penalty layered on top.

## Growth without a productivity mandate

“Grow in your own way” means the game recognizes different forms of development. It does not require a numerical increase every day.

Valid growth may include:

- learning a skill;
- repairing an object;
- preserving history;
- improving a room;
- listening to somebody;
- setting a boundary;
- recovering energy;
- leaving an unhealthy direction;
- trying something and deciding it is not for you;
- remaining stable during a difficult period.

## Design test

A player-facing system should answer these questions:

1. What real cause does this mechanic represent?
2. Can repeated low-value actions be compressed?
3. Can the player ignore it without an artificial punishment?
4. Is “no” a real outcome?
5. Does the system create possibilities or recurring chores?
6. Can rest and change of direction remain valid?
7. Does the world continue coherently without player participation?

If a system exists mainly to create retention pressure, it does not belong in this branch.
